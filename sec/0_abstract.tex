\begin{abstract}
Physical understanding from visual inputs is a fundamental ability required for successful robotic interaction with real-world environments. Although several recent works address improving spatial reasoning in vision-language models (VLMs), there is a need to evaluate and improve their physical understanding capabilities on a wider variety of tasks. To this end, we propose a benchmark dataset of 325 real-world image-question samples covering four estimation tasks - weight, volume, stability margin, and deformation-aware fitting. Samples have precisely-measured ground truth values, mostly quantitative, and involve scenes that make estimation challenging but feasible. To perform well on our benchmark requires a VLM not only to be adept at reasoning about geometry but also to have semantic, prior knowledge of how objects are typically constructed and used in everyday environments. The three frontier models we evaluated on our dataset showed significant prediction errors across categories, though they performed comparable to human-baseline. We carry out three control studies involving blurred visual input, cropped object of interest, and text-only prompt to ascertain that VLMs do well on our benchmark when they actually reason from provided visual data rather than simply rely on information memorized during training. Finally, we take weight estimation as our prototypical task and propose a scalable pipeline to generate a teacher-distilled dataset with approximate ground-truth to fine-tune open-weight models. We observe performance improvements of Qwen 3.5 9B and Gemma 4 31B VLMs on our weight estimation benchmark when fine-tuned with Gemini 4 Argon distilled dataset.
\end{abstract}
